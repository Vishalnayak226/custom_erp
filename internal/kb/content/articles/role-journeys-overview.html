Which journey is mine #

The articles in this section are organised by job, not by the role record on your account. Those are two different things and it is worth being clear about which is which before you read further.

The four roles a business starts with #

RoleReaches
AdminThe whole business: every module, store, approval, user, role and setting
Store ManagerStore operations, stock, orders, procurement, local reports
CashierThe till, its returns, and customer lookup
SupplierOnly Supplier Submissions, and only its own company's

Super Admin is the platform provider's account, not the business's. It adds only the developer tools: Database Schema Design, Extension Hooks and System Status.

Anything beyond these four is a role your administrator creates on Settings » Roles. A role named after a shipped template - Picker, Warehouse Manager, Accounts Payable, Accounts Receivable, Accountant, HR Manager, Employee Self-Service, Auditor, Integrator or Merchandiser - starts with that template's access; any other role is granted read, create, update and delete per record type. The system fails closed: a record type with no grant row for a role is denied to that role, never allowed by default.

Note

If a journey describes a screen you cannot open, that is your role, not a fault or a missing feature. Ask an administrator, and point them at the record types the journey names.

Reading a journey #

Each one is written as a day rather than a feature list: what you do first, what you do when something is wrong, and what you check before you go home. They assume you have already read What this system is and can find your way around.

Where a journey needs detail it does not have room for, it links to the module handbook for that area rather than repeating it.
