Open a shop and make your first sale #

Every other article is per-screen. This one is the opposite: one continuous path from an empty system to money in the till, so you can see how the pieces connect rather than reading about them one at a time. Allow about half an hour.

The same walkthrough is section 13 of the user guide. The guide is the master copy; this article follows it step for step.

Important

One login is enough while self-approval is allowed: whoever raises the purchase order may approve it too. A new client starts with Who can approve their own documents set to Nobody. Then you need a second login to approve the order - ask your administrator for one before you start.

1. Create the place you trade from #

Setup » Core » Location » New Location. Give it a code (MAIN), a name, and set Type = Store. Save.

This is the one record that makes a shop real to the rest of the system. Every transaction points at a Location.

2. Create the supplier you buy from #

Procurement » Vendors » New Vendor. Name and contact details. Save.

3. Create something to sell #

Setup » Inventory » Item » New Item. Fill in the name and code, and - this is the step people skip - the HSN Code and GST Rate. Both are required: the system refuses to save without them, because it cannot price a sale it cannot tax. Save.

Selling something that isn't taxed? A 0 in GST Rate on its own is still rejected, because the system cannot tell it apart from a rate nobody has filled in yet. Set Tax Treatment instead. It sits next to GST Rate, in the Price and tax section.

Tax TreatmentUse it forGST Rate
TaxableEverything ordinary. What you get if you leave the field alone.Must be greater than 0
ExemptGoods exempted by notification - fresh produce, unbranded grain.Set to 0 for you
Nil-RatedGoods whose tariff rate is genuinely 0% - salt, certain cereals.Set to 0 for you
Zero-RatedExports and SEZ supplies made under LUT or bond.Set to 0 for you

HSN Code is still required on all four - it goes on the invoice whatever the rate is, and the nil and exempt part of GSTR-1 is reported HSN-wise too. You cannot have it both ways either: an item with a non-taxable treatment may not carry a GST Rate above 0.

You now have an item with zero stock. That is expected.

4. Order some stock #

Procurement » Purchase Order. Pick your vendor, target warehouse and location, enter the amount, and click Create Draft.

You do not type a PO number. It is issued on save, from the numbering rules your administrator configured.

Click Submit for Approval.

5. Approve it (as the other user) #

Sign in with your second account, open Financial Accounting » Approvals, find the purchase order and click Approve.

Tip

If Approve is refused, you are signed in as the person who raised it. That is the check working, not a fault. Use the other account.

6. Receive the stock #

Back as the first user: Procurement » Goods Receipt. Click + New Goods Receipt, pick the Vendor, tick your approved PO and click Load ticked POs - or use Receive goods on the PO's row. Confirm the quantities that actually arrived, type the vendor's invoice number, date and total, and click Save receipt. Then work through the steps: Verify, Check prices, type the MRP and Save and post each one - or Post all when everything matches.

Now check Inventory » Stock on Hand. The quantity should have gone up.

Warning

If stock did not move, stop here. Everything downstream depends on this step having worked, and continuing will only produce a second confusing failure.

7. Open the till #

POS » POS / Billing. Check that the store at the top right of the screen is your shop - click it and pick the shop if you work at several. Click Open Session and enter the cash physically in the drawer. While the session is open you stay at that shop.

Tip

Shop not in the list at the top? Either you have not been given it - an administrator adds it in Users » Stores - or it is set to Sellable = No. Open Setup » Core » Location, find it, set Sellable = Yes and save.

8. Sell something #

Scan the item, or type part of its name, press Enter and pick it from the list. Choose a payment mode. For Cash, type what the customer handed you into Cash tendered and read off the Change due. Click Complete Sale.

The till now freezes on the finished bill: the bill number, the lines, the total collected and the change. Click Print Receipt to see it on screen; Print there sends it straight to the till printer where one has been set up, and otherwise through the browser's print dialog. Click New Sale (Reset) when you are ready for the next customer.

9. Check that everything moved on its own #

You should not have to tell any other screen about that sale. Confirm it:

CheckWhereWhat you should see
Stock went downInventory » Stock on HandQuantity reduced by what you sold
The sale was recordedReports » Sales » Sales Register, then RunYour sale, with its total
The books balancedFinancial Accounting » Trial BalanceSet As Of Date to today - debits equal credits, and the status reads "Balanced trial ledger"
The money is owed to the vendorReports » Procurement » Vendor Ledger, then RunYour purchase order against that vendor

This table is the point of the whole exercise. Four screens nobody touched now agree with each other.

10. Close the till #

Back on POS / Billing, click Close Session and enter the counted cash. The system shows expected, counted and the variance.

The expected cash is the X-report's own figure, so a close never disagrees with the X-report. When the till's bills took card or UPI, the close also asks for the card terminal's and the UPI app's totals and keeps their difference from what the bills say. Reports » Sales & POS » Z-report (till close) shows each closed till afterwards.

That is the skeleton #

Buy, receive, sell - and the accounting follows by itself. Every other feature hangs off this loop. From here:
