Finance & Tax #

Use fixed assets for asset records and lifecycle actions, and expense claims for submission, approval, verification and settlement.

Everything in this handbook sits on one mechanism: PostDoubleEntry, the single function every posted document in this system goes through to reach the general ledger. It takes a balanced set of debits and credits keyed by account code, refuses to post into a closed accounting period, and is idempotent by construction — a repeat call carrying the exact same posting key (shaped <DocType>:<DocID>:<PURPOSE>) replays as a silent no-op instead of posting twice, which is what makes a dropped network response safe to retry. Everything else in this module — invoices, payments, revaluation, manual journal entries — is a caller of that one function.

The Finance screen #

One sidebar entry each: Work to review, Trial Balance, Chart of Accounts, Accounting Periods. This is deliberately a small screen — most of what a controller actually reads regularly lives in Reports → Finance instead (25 registered reports as of this writing; see Reports below). Manual entries have their own screen, Financial Accounting → Journal Vouchers (see Manual journal entries). A few real, working finance actions still have no screen at all yet — see What is not here yet before assuming something doesn't exist just because it isn't here.

Approvals inbox #

What it is

One list of every document waiting for your sign-off, from every module: purchase orders, payments over a limit, write-offs, price changes and more.

Why it matters

A rule decides which documents need a second person. Nothing waiting for you is buried in its own screen, and every decision is recorded with who made it.

Use case

A buyer raises a ₹2,40,000 purchase order, above the limit that needs a manager. It appears in the manager's Approvals. The manager checks the amount and location, clicks Approve, and the buyer can send the order.

How to do it

  1. Open Financial Accounting → Approvals.
  2. Check each row's record type, reference, amount and location.
  3. Click Approve or Reject on its row. A reject asks for an optional reason.
  4. To decide many at once, tick them and click Approve ticked or Reject ticked.

Password resets and client data removal are decided one at a time. Which documents need approval, and who may approve, is set under Security and approvals (handbook).

Chart of Accounts #

Every GL account has a code, a name, and a fixed type (Asset, Liability, Equity, Revenue, Expense) that decides its normal balance side. New accounts a feature needs (e.g. a new statutory-liability account) are seeded by that feature's own migration — there's no "never touch this" account, but adding one outside a migration means understanding which side of the ledger it normally sits on before anything posts to it.

Posting dimensions #

A posting can optionally be tagged with up to four whole-posting dimensions — Cost Center, Department, Entity, and Project — each applying to every line one PostDoubleEntry call inserts, not per line within it (the debit/credit maps already aggregate by account code before they reach this function, so a per-line dimension isn't representable without restructuring that). These dimensions are what the dimensioned P&L reports (Cost Center P&L, Department P&L, Project P&L) and the entity/ consolidated trial balance reports filter and group by.

Cost center, entity and department come from the document (Stage 93). Every Location has its own cost center — made with it, named after it — and belongs to one legal entity (both required; Admin Guide B.3.0.3b). A posting with none of its own takes:

  • cost center and entity from its document's location: POS sales, sales invoices, GRNs, purchase returns, consumption, expense claims and assets directly; credit notes, vendor invoices, debit / vendor credit notes, refunds and payslips through the invoice, cart, GRN, return or employee they come from;
  • department from the document (an expense claim's), else its parent (a payslip's employee), else the user who raised it — every user has a department from the day they are created. A journal voucher carries only the department picked on it.

Anything picked on the document is kept, and an Inactive record is skipped rather than refusing the posting. So the Cost Center P&L reads store by store, the Department P&L team by team, and the trial balance company by company, without anyone tagging a sale by hand.

Accounting periods and closing #

What it is

An accounting period is a named date range. Close locks it so nothing more can post inside it.

Why it matters

A closed period cannot be changed by a late posting. Five signed close tasks stand behind each close, and reopening needs a reason and a second person.

Use case

You create a period for April. Each close task's owner signs it off with evidence, then you close it. A correction found in May is a reopen request, another accountant's approval and a reversing entry.

How to do it

  1. On Financial Accounting → Accounting Periods, click + New Period: name, start and end date.
  2. Click Close tasks, assign an owner to each of the five tasks, and each owner clicks Sign off with the evidence.
  3. Click Close period. If a pre-close check still fails, give the reason.
  4. To correct a closed period, click Request reopen with a reason. Another Accountant, Admin or Super Admin approves it in Approvals.

Create a period (Financial Accounting → Accounting Periods, + New Period) with a name and a date range — a range overlapping any existing period (Open or Closed) is refused outright, since an ambiguous "which period is today in" question is worse than refusing the second period.

Close needs all five close tasks signed off by their owners (Bank reconciliation, Stock valuation, Payables, Receivables, Marketplace / POS settlement), and is refused while a till is still open or offline sales are waiting to sync. Once a period is Closed, any posting dated inside it is refused (FIN-0260), checked from the database's own clock, not the app server's. Reopening needs a written reason and a second person: Request reopen waits in Approvals, the period then starts a new close round, and corrections are posted as new reversing entries, never by editing history.

Before closing, four read-only pre-close checks show which ones pass. A failing one does not block the close, but the close then asks why and keeps the reason in the period's close log:

CheckWhat it means if it fails
Trial balance is balancedSomething's debits and credits don't match as of the period's end date.
No documents awaiting approvalAt least one document is still sitting Pending Approval.
No vendor invoices in mismatch holdA 3-way-match discrepancy (GRN vs. PO vs. invoice) hasn't been resolved.
No unmatched bank statement lines dated in this periodA reconciliation is incomplete for this period.

A backdated posting into an already-closed period is possible, but only through an explicit, named exception: a BackdatedPostingRequest naming the exact document/date it authorizes, itself an approval-gated document. There is no blanket override — each one is scoped to one specific posting.

Trial Balance #

The Trial Balance page (Financial Accounting → General ledger) is as-of-a-date, not "since the beginning of time" — pick a date and it shows every account's balance as it stood then, optionally filtered by Cost Center/Department/Entity. A balanced ledger shows total debits equal to total credits; if they don't, that's exactly what the pre-close checklist above is designed to catch before you try to close the period it happened in.

Accounts Receivable: Sales Invoices #

What it is

Sales Invoices lists invoices with Post and Settle as two separate actions. Posting books the receivable. Settling records that it was collected.

Why it matters

Once an invoice is Approved or Paid its money is fixed. You correct it with a credit or debit note, not by editing the amounts.

Use case

A ₹1,01,500 invoice is raised for a customer in Pune. You Post it to book the receivable, and Settle it when the payment actually comes in.

How to do it

  1. On Financial Accounting → Sales Invoice, open the invoice or click + New Sales Invoice.
  2. Click Post to book the receivable.
  3. Click Settle once the money is collected. To change a posted amount, raise a credit or debit note.

Sidebar → Sales Invoices: list, then Post and Settle as two separate, explicit actions on an invoice — posting books the receivable (Dr Accounts Receivable / Cr Revenue); settling records that it was actually collected. A deferred-revenue invoice posts to a deferred liability account instead of straight to revenue, and a background worker recognizes it into revenue on schedule — see the Deferred Revenue Roll-Forward report to track one in progress.

A posted document's money is fixed (FIN-0844, 47.16.4). Once a sales invoice is Approved or Paid, a credit, debit or vendor credit note is Posted, a journal voucher is Posted, or a vendor invoice has any payment, a save may not change the amounts, tax, currency or exchange rate it posted with. Nor may a save mark it Cancelled, Draft, Rejected, Void or Reversed — that would leave its receivable and GST in the ledger while the document said it was gone. Correct it with a credit or debit note (CGST s.34) or the document's own reversal, each posting its own entry. An Approved goods receipt is held the same way: a save cannot cancel it; it is reversed with a purchase return (49.5.3).

E-invoice (IRN) #

For a GSTIN that must e-invoice (turnover above ₹5 crore), every posted B2B sales invoice and customer credit note gets its IRN from the government's IRP. An admin sets each GSTIN up once under Financial Accounting → E-Invoice Settings (on/off, send automatically or not, turnover band, document types, credentials); only the test provider exists until the IRIS sandbox is connected. Accountants work the queue in Financial Accounting → E-Invoice (IRN): what needs fixing (HSN, unit, PIN code, GSTIN, a number over 16 characters) is listed with the fix, Validate rebuilds it, Submit sends it, Unknown means the IRP did not answer and the document is looked up before anything is sent again. Once it has an IRN, its tax details are locked; Cancel IRN within 24 hours needs a second Accountant's approval, after that issue a credit note. B2C sales are never sent. The invoice print shows the IRN, Ack and the signed QR, or IRN PENDING. Outage path: generate it on the portal, then Enter portal IRN (verified by a second person). Reports: E-Invoice / IRN Status Register, Error / Retry Report, IRN Portal Manual Upload Report.

Accounts Payable: Vendor Invoices #

What it is

The vendor's bill, checked against the purchase order and the goods actually received before anything is paid.

Why it matters

You pay only for what you ordered and accepted. A bill that does not agree is held, so an overcharge is caught before the money leaves.

Use case

A vendor bills ₹59,000 but the goods receipt accepted ₹53,100 worth after a damaged carton was rejected. Match puts the bill on hold and shows both figures. The accountant asks the vendor for a corrected bill instead of paying the difference.

How to do it

  1. Open Financial Accounting → Vendor Invoice, click + New Vendor Invoice and enter the bill against its purchase order and goods receipt.
  2. Click Match. It compares the bill with the accepted goods and the purchase order.
  3. If it is Matched, click Pay and enter the amount; less than the balance is a part-payment.
  4. If it is on hold, correct the bill, or click Override & Pay and give a reason; the payment then waits for approval.

Advance sets an advance already paid to this vendor against the bill. Pay w/ TDS picks the TDS section and withholds it; it is offered only before any part of the bill is paid, because TDS is withheld on the whole bill.

Payment Proposals: paying many bills at once #

What it is

A payment run: a batch of matched vendor bills paid together in one step.

Why it matters

Paying on a fixed day keeps the cash plan simple. Each bill in the run is paid exactly as if it were paid on its own, so the books are the same either way.

Use case

Every Friday the accountant ticks the bills due that week, creates one proposal and executes it. One bill fails because it was already part-paid by hand; the result names that bill and the rest are paid.

How to do it

  1. Open Financial Accounting → Payment Proposals.
  2. Click + New Proposal and tick the matched bills to pay.
  3. Click Create Proposal from Selected.
  4. Click Execute on the proposal and confirm.

Any bill that could not be paid is listed with its reason; the others are paid.

Warning

The bank payment file has no button yet. GET /api/v1/finance/payment-proposal/{id}/payment-file works but nothing in the app calls it, so producing the file your bank needs takes a direct API call today. The UTRs that come back are recorded from the screen: load the bank's response file in Bank files.

Debit and credit notes #

What it is

Notes that correct a posted bill or invoice: a debit note claims money back from a vendor, a vendor credit note records the vendor's own credit, and a customer credit note gives money back to a customer.

Why it matters

A posted document's money cannot be edited. A note books the correction as its own entry, which is what GST law expects and what an auditor can follow.

Use case

A customer returns a ₹4,720 kurta after the invoice was posted. The accountant raises a customer credit note against that invoice for ₹4,720 and posts it. The output GST and the receivable go down by the right amounts.

How to do it

  1. Open Financial Accounting → Debit Notes, Vendor Credit Notes or Customer Credit Notes.
  2. Click + New Debit Note, + New Vendor Credit Note or + New Credit Note and fill in the note against its bill or invoice.
  3. Click Post and confirm. Posting books the entry at once and cannot be undone.
  4. Click Print for the copy you send.

A goods receipt billed above the purchase order price raises its debit note by itself.

Bank Reconciliation #

What it is

Each line of the bank statement matched to the entry in your books that it pays or receives.

Why it matters

It proves the books agree with the bank. Anything left over is a missing entry, a bank charge or a mistake, and it is found while it is still fresh.

Use case

At month end 212 of 215 lines match on Exact match. One is a ₹590 bank charge, so the accountant posts the charge and matches it. One customer payment covers two invoices: Find match, tick both, Match selected. The last is unknown: Raise exception with an owner and a reason.

How to do it

  1. Open Financial Accounting → Bank Reconciliation and pick the bank account and dates.
  2. Load the statement with Statement Lines / Import CSV, or under Bank files.
  3. Click Exact match: same amount, within 3 days.
  4. For what is left, click Rule match, set the amount tolerance and date window, and click Run rule match.
  5. On each open line, click Find match, tick the book entries and click Match selected (or Part match when they cover only part of the line). Otherwise use Explain or Raise exception.

Show narrows the list to open lines, the exception queue, matched or explained lines. Unmatch undoes a match and asks why. Settlement file and Match card / UPI match the card and UPI payouts to their bank credits.

Bank files: statements and bank responses #

Bank Reconciliation → Bank files has two loaders.

  • Statement. Pick the bank account, the format (CSV or MT940) and the file. Every line gets a stable id, so loading the same file twice adds nothing; the result lists what was loaded and what was skipped.
  • Bank response. Enter an Executed payment proposal, pick the bank account and the bank's CSV (payment_reference, utr, status, reason). A paid row records its UTR; a rejected row raises a bank exception with the bank's reason and reverses nothing. A reference the proposal does not hold is listed, not fatal.

Both need finance.post.

Multi-currency and FX #

A document in a foreign currency posts in both that currency and the base currency, booked at the rate in effect when it posted. Two further pieces close the loop:

  • Realised gain/loss on settlement — when a foreign-currency invoice is actually paid/settled at a different rate than it was booked at, the difference posts automatically to a Realised FX Gain or Loss account.
  • Period-end revaluation (GET/POST /api/v1/finance/fx-revaluation, the GET previews and the POST commits — deliberately two different verbs on the same URL, not a dry_run flag on one POST, so a retry or prefetch can never accidentally book a second adjustment) restates every still-open foreign-currency balance at the period-end closing rate and books the movement to an Unrealised FX Gain/Loss account. Open/committed balances are revalued — commitments like a Purchase Order, Sales Order or RFQ are not, since revaluing a commitment would post a gain against a control account holding no matching real balance. Revaluing the same document twice for the same as-of date is refused with a named reason, not silently skipped, since "nothing to do" and "already done" mean different things to whoever is closing the books.

Warning

FX revaluation has no screen either. The preview/commit split above is a real, deliberate safety design, but there is nowhere in the app to reach it — confirmed by grep, zero matches for fx-revaluation in public/app.js. A period-end revaluation needs a direct API call today.

Three registered reports read multi-currency data without needing their own screen: Open FX Exposure, FX Gain/Loss Register, and Trial Balance in Presentation Currency — all reachable from Reports → Finance like any other report.

Troubleshooting #

A posting is refused: "transaction date falls within closed accounting period." This is FIN-0260 — either post with today's (open-period) date instead, or have an admin create an approved BackdatedPostingRequest naming this exact document and date first.

The pre-close checklist shows the trial balance as unbalanced. Something posted an unequal debit/credit somewhere — this should not happen through any normal document action (PostDoubleEntry refuses an unbalanced call outright), so treat it as worth investigating rather than working around.

A vendor invoice won't pay — it's stuck in mismatch hold. A 3-way-match discrepancy between the GRN, PO and invoice needs resolving first; this is also one of the four pre-close checklist items, so it will block a clean close if left unresolved.

Need to generate a bank payment file, but there's no button. See the warning under Payment Proposals above: it is a real endpoint reachable only via a direct API call today. UTRs are recorded by loading the bank's response in Bank files.

Manual journal entries (Journal Vouchers) #

What it is

A Journal Voucher is a manual accounting entry for something no other screen makes, such as an accrual, a correction or an opening balance.

Why it matters

You can only save it when the bar shows Balanced. Approval by another user is what posts it, and a posted voucher is never edited: you Reverse it instead.

Use case

At month end you accrue ₹45,000 of electricity charges. You enter the Date and Narration, add one debit and one credit line, Save as Draft, and Submit for Approval so another user can approve it.

For an entry no other screen makes — an accrual, a correction, an opening balance — use Financial Accounting → Journal Vouchers:

A new journal voucher: debit and credit lines that must balance before it can be saved

How to do it

  1. Click + New Journal Voucher. Enter the Date (the voucher books on that date, even if it is approved later) and a Narration; Cost Center and Department are optional.
  2. Add one row per account with + Add line — an Account and either a Debit or a Credit. The bar shows Balanced when the totals match; only then can you Save as Draft.
  3. On the list, click Submit for Approval on its row. Another user approves it on Approvals; approval is what posts it.
  4. A posted voucher is never edited: Reverse makes a new voucher with every line swapped, which goes through approval the same way. An approved voucher whose posting failed shows Retry Post.

The full steps are in the User Guide (§9B, Journal Vouchers).

What is not here yet #

These real, tested, GL-posting actions still have no screen, confirmed by a search of public/ (2026-10-24):

  • Recurring journal templates — the journal-voucher API can save a voucher as a recurring template; the Journal Vouchers screen lists only ordinary vouchers and has no button to make or run a template.
  • Intercompany Transactions — cross-entity mirrored postings (POST /api/v1/finance/intercompany-transaction), used by the entity/ consolidated trial balance reports, are API-only.
  • Landed Cost Vouchers — allocating freight/duty/other charges onto landed cost (POST /api/v1/finance/landed-cost-voucher) is API-only.
  • FX revaluation and bank-payment-file generation — see the warnings above.

In every one of these cases the underlying feature is real, tested, and already how the reports listed above get their data — this is a coverage gap in the frontend, not a stub in the backend.
