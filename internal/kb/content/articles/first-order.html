Your first order, end to end #

This walks one order through every screen it touches. Do it once on a test product and the rest of the application stops looking like a list of unrelated menus.

Before you start #

You need one product with stock. Check Inventory and confirm the product shows a positive available-to-sell figure at your location. If it shows zero, receive some stock first - an order for stock you do not have will be accepted but will not allocate.

1. Place the order #

  1. Open OMS » Orders and click New Order.
  2. Fill in the customer's name, phone and address.
  3. Click + Add item, pick the product and set the quantity.
  4. Click Create Order.

A channel order needs nothing by hand: the connector brings it in as the same kind of order. A sale at POS ends at its bill and does not come through these steps.

2. Watch it allocate #

Open OMS » Orders and find your order. Its status tells you whether stock has been committed to it:

StatusMeaning
ReservedStock is held for this order at one location; it is ready to pick
On HoldSomething stops it; the order shows why, such as Waiting for stock

An order Waiting for stock is also on the Allocation pending tile on the same screen. Most often no location has enough available-to-sell stock: receive stock and the order can be reserved.

3. Pick, pack and dispatch #

A channel order first waits on OMS → Pending Orders until someone accepts it (an order keyed in by staff skips this). Then each step has its own page: Pick (scan the units), Pack (scan them into the box, then Pack, bill and label - the OMS bill and the courier AWB in one go) and Ship (manifest, gate pass, hand over). Each step records who did it and when.

Tip

Click Expedite on the order's page to push it to the front of the pick queue. The queue itself puts it first, not just a label.

4. Invoice #

The customer's bill is the OMS bill made at Pack; its number is the GST invoice number. Handing the manifest over makes the ERP bill - a posted Sales Invoice with the same number - so you do not raise one by hand. Both carry the tax treatment resolved from the product's own HSN code and the places of supply - you do not enter tax rates by hand anywhere.

What to look at when something stalls #

  • OMS » Exceptions lists every order stuck for a reason the system knows.
  • SLA breaches lists tasks older than their threshold.
  • The order detail screen shows lines, reservations, tasks, shipments, invoices, returns, refunds, notifications and the audit trail in one place - start there before hunting through modules.
