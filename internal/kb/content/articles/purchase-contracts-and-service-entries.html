Purchase contracts and service entries #

A contract with a vendor #

What it is

A purchase contract fixes one vendor's rates for a period, with an optional cap on what you may buy under it.

Why it matters

Every PO released under it is held to the deal: no rate above the contract's, no item off it, no date outside it and no release past the cap. The negotiated terms are enforced on each order instead of remembered.

Use case

You agree a year's rate for packing tape with one supplier: ₹38 a roll, up to ₹5,00,000. Each month's order is released from the contract, the rate fills itself in, and the contract shows how much of the cap is used.

A new contract: vendor, kind, validity and cap, then each item's rate

How to do it

  1. Open Procurement » Contracts and click New contract.
  2. Pick the Vendor and the Kind, set Valid from and Valid to, and optionally a Store and a Cap value.
  3. Click Add item for each item and give its rate (and an optional cap quantity).
  4. Click Save. The contract is a Draft.
  5. A purchase-order approver opens it and clicks Activate. The terms are now locked.

Buying under it #

  1. Open an Active contract and click Release PO. The new PO opens with the vendor, store and contract filled in.
  2. Add lines. Leave the rate blank to take the contract rate.
  3. Submit the PO for approval as usual.

The contract refuses a rate above its own, an item not on it, a PO dated outside its validity, another vendor's PO, and a release that would pass the cap.

The contract shows Released ... of ... against its cap, and lists each release PO. A release counts once it is submitted; a cancelled one gives its value back. Close ends a contract early.

Paying for a service #

What it is

A service entry records work a vendor has done against a service PO: a repair, a maintenance visit, a consultant's days.

Why it matters

A service has no goods to receive, so the service entry stands in for the goods receipt. The cost is accrued once, when an approver accepts the work, and the vendor's bill is matched against accepted work, not against the PO.

Use case

You order 12 monthly AC services. After the March visit the store enters 1 service done and the manager accepts it. The vendor's March bill matches the one accepted visit; a bill for two would be held.

A new service entry: pick the service PO, then key what was done this time

How to do it

  1. On New purchase order, set Type to Service. A service PO is never received on a goods receipt, and no stock moves.
  2. When work is done, open Procurement » Service Entry, click New service entry, pick the service PO and enter the quantity done on each line. You cannot enter more than was ordered, less what is already accepted.
  3. Click Save. An approver opens the entry and clicks Accept. The cost is accrued once; clicking Accept again changes nothing.
  4. On the vendor's bill, set the PO and the Service entry. The bill is matched against the accepted work. A bill for more than that is held.

Tip

On the PO list, a service PO's Receive goods button reads Service entry and opens this screen.
